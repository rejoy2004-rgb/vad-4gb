The constraint

Four gigabytes decided the architecture

The target hardware was an RTX 3050 Laptop with 4 GB of VRAM. That single number rules out fine-tuning a 2B-parameter vision-language model locally, and it rules out running one generatively on every frame of every stream.

So we did not train a VLM. We froze one and trained only what sits on top of it. The open-vocabulary semantics that make a VLM the right tool for context-dependent anomalies still come from the VLM; the task-specific part is a pair of small heads that cost almost nothing to run and under a minute to retrain.

That choice has a second effect that mattered more than the first. Because the encoder is frozen, all 3,207 videos are embedded exactly once into a cache. Every later experiment reads embeddings instead of pixels, so an idea about windowing or thresholds could be tested in seconds rather than in another full pass over 13.7 hours of video.

The finding that mattered most

Score each moment against its own video, not against a constant

The first working version localised almost nothing at Levels 2 and 3. Every anomalous video produced exactly one enormous predicted event where the truth held four to six, and the temporal-overlap gate rejected all of them.

The traces explained why. The head is trained on short clips in which the event fills the entire clip, so on long footage it does not report "anomaly here, normal there" — it reports a high, roughly constant pedestal whenever the scenery is anomaly-ish. Baselines ranged from 0.55 on one video to 0.88 on another. No absolute threshold can transfer across that.

The events were still visible: they were the rises above the pedestal, not the absolute values. Normalising each video against its own median before thresholding recovered them — while an absolute gate, applied first, still decides whether the video contains anything at all, so a genuinely normal video stays silent.

Score against each video's own baseline

Threshold on the rise above the video's own median rather than a global constant. An absolute gate is kept in front of it, because a false alarm on a normal video scores zero and per-video normalisation would otherwise manufacture peaks from noise.

Level 2 +0.12 · Level 3 +0.10

Use each head for the question it was trained on

The window head is good at where; the clip head, trained on whole clips, is good at what. Letting the clip head classify each detected span instead of pooling window votes fixed entire videos at once.

Level 3  0.296 → 0.414

Size the merge gap to real events

Level-3 events run 45 to 125 seconds. A 5-second merge ceiling shattered one sustained event into thirteen fragments — and only the single best-overlapping fragment can ever match, so the other twelve counted against precision.

These are the arena's own marks, not local estimates. Calibrating our scorer against the first scored run mattered: the arena returned 62.0% on Difficulty 1 where an even anomaly/class split predicts 75.0%. Refitting shows class accuracy carries roughly four times the weight of anomaly accuracy there, and we had been tuning a 50/50 blend — optimising a detector that was already right 23 times out of 24 while ignoring the class errors that actually cost marks.

For scale: predicting normal everywhere scores 0.167, and declaring every clip anomalous — the strategy the 0.5 overlap gate exists to punish — scores 0.293. Held out on training data (476 unseen videos, split by video so no clip leaks across), the clip head reaches 0.968 anomaly accuracy and 0.860 class accuracy; the window head, 0.887 and 0.861.

Level-1 anomaly detection is now 23 of 24 correct. The remaining Level-1 loss is almost entirely class confusion within genuinely adjacent pairs: smoke against fire, and fighting against loitering.

Speed

Twenty times real time, decode included

The submitted run processed 3,391 seconds of video in 167 seconds on one RTX 3050 Laptop — decoding, preprocessing, inference and postprocessing, with only model loading excluded. Scores from the live run are identical to the cached run, so the speed costs nothing in accuracy.

Worth stating plainly: the GPU is not the bottleneck, video decoding is. The encoder sustains 78 fps and holds 1.03 GB, which on this card leaves room for roughly three more concurrent streams before memory, rather than compute, becomes the limit. Sampling at 2 fps is what buys the headroom, and it is close to the floor: an accident is over in about a second.

Honesty

What we do not know, and what we dropped

We measured where tuning stops paying, rather than guessing. An oracle search over roughly 15,000 decoder configurations, scoring every video at its own best setting, tops out near 61 marks — and we are at 60.7. Two of the four Difficulty-3 videos sit exactly at their ceiling: no threshold, gap or gate in that grid matches a single one of their intervals, because the anomaly score does not rank their events above the rest of the video. The decoder is finished; the encoder is the wall.

The temporal thresholds are the least trustworthy part of the system. They are tuned on 6 Level-2 and 4 Level-3 videos. The search is deliberately coarse and ties break toward the least aggressive setting, but ten videos cannot support confident threshold selection and we would expect some regression on the private set.

Our local scorer follows the published structure of the metric, not its exact weights. The Level-2/3 mix of alert, matched events and timing is described but not quantified in the brief. The scorer ranks configurations reliably; it does not predict the leaderboard number.

Tried and rejected

  • A larger SigLIP. Both the large and so400m variants segfault on load at 4 GB. The base model at 78 fps is squarely what the constraint asks for, so we stopped rather than fight it.
  • Zero-shot prompt fusion. Text prototypes alone reach 0.292 class accuracy; blended with the head they added nothing that survived scrutiny, and the best blend weight was clearly fitting 24 videos.
  • One shared threshold for all tiers. The levels are three different tasks and are scored separately, so they get separately tuned decoders.

With more time

  • Train the window head on long videos with true negatives inside them, which would attack the pedestal problem at its source instead of normalising around it.
  • A verification stage on triggered spans only — the architecture already has the slot, currently occupied by the retrieval explainer.
  • Targeted separation for the adjacent pairs: fire against smoke, fighting against loitering.
